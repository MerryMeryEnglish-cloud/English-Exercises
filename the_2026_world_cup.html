The 2026 World Cup

A Summer to Remember

In the summer of 2026, the men's World Cup was bigger than ever before. For the first time, 48 national teams took part, and the tournament was hosted by three countries: the United States, Canada and Mexico. Some people feared that adding more teams would lead to many one-sided matches. Although there were some heavy defeats, several smaller nations proved that they deserved to be there.

The best example was Cape Verde, a small island country off the coast of West Africa. It was playing in its first World Cup, and its opening match was against Spain, the European champions. Very few people expected the newcomers to get anything from the game. However, they defended bravely and the match finished 0–0. It was not just luck. Cape Verde went on to draw with Uruguay and Saudi Arabia, finished second in their group and qualified for the knockout stage.

Spain, meanwhile, recovered from that disappointing start. As the tournament progressed, they became extremely difficult to beat. Their success was not only based on attacking talent. Their defence was outstanding: in eight matches, they conceded just one goal, against Belgium in the quarter-finals.

The final took place on 19 July at MetLife Stadium, near New York. Spain faced Argentina, the defending champions, led by Lionel Messi. Spain kept possession of the ball for long periods, so Messi's teammates found it hard to pass to him. Neither side managed to score in normal time. Argentina's situation became even more complicated when Enzo Fernández was sent off, leaving them with only ten players.

The decisive moment arrived in the 106th minute, during extra time. Pedro Porro sent a cross towards the far post, Nico Williams headed the ball down, and Ferran Torres struck it into the net with his left foot. The goal gave Spain a 1–0 victory and their second World Cup title, 16 years after their first one in 2010.

There were individual prizes too. Spain's midfielder Rodri won the Golden Ball for the best player of the tournament, while his teammate Unai Simón was named the best goalkeeper. Another Spaniard, defender Pau Cubarsí, received the award for the best young player. France's Kylian Mbappé finished as the top scorer with ten goals. His team, however, ended in fourth place after losing an incredible match against England 6–4. Bukayo Saka scored three of England's goals.

For many footballers, the tournament offered a valuable lesson. Famous names can attract attention, but organisation, patience and teamwork often make the difference. Cape Verde showed that an underdog can compete with a giant, and Spain showed that a strong defence can be just as important as a brilliant attack.
