📐 Grammar Focus: The "Used to" Family

Students often confuse these three structures. Let's break them down clearly:

1. Used to + Infinitive

Use: For past habits or states that are no longer true now.

"We used to talk every single day."

2. Be used to + -ing / Noun

Use: To be accustomed to something; it is normal for you.

"Now she is used to living without him."

3. Get used to + -ing / Noun

Use: The process of becoming accustomed to something new.

"It takes time to get used to being single again."

💡 Key Expressions & British Pronunciation (UK)

Clicking Listen will read out both the target expression and its example sentence in authentic British accent.

1. "Somebody that I used to know"

Meaning: A person you had a close relationship with in the past, but who has now become a stranger.

Example: "We shared an apartment for years, but now we do not speak; she is just somebody that I used to know."

2. "Cut somebody out"

Meaning: To stop communicating with someone completely or exclude them from your life.

Example: "After constant arguments, he decided to cut her out of his life completely."

3. "Treat your data like you had no change"

Meaning: Acting coldly after a split, completely disregarding shared history as if the person owes nothing or never mattered.

Example: "After the breakup, he would treat your data like you had no change, deleting every message and acting like they were complete strangers."

4. "Stoop low"

Meaning: Behaving in a petty, mean, or dishonorable way that is below one's usual standards.

Example: "He did not need to spread rumors about her friends; it was unnecessary to stoop so low."

5. "Records"

Meaning: Vinyl music albums representing shared possessions divided after a breakup.

Example: "When they moved out, their friends came over and collected all their vinyl records."
