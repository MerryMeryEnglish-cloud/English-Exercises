Snow-White and Rose-Red

Merry Mery English • Interactive Reading Unit

Story Summary

Read or listen to the tale (~280 words)

A poor widow lived in a cottage with two gentle daughters: quiet Snow-White and lively Rose-Red. Once, after sleeping peacefully near a steep precipice in the dark forest, their mother explained that a guardian angel had protected them from falling.

During cold winter evenings, Snow-White hung a polished brass kettle on the hob while their mother read aloud. One freezing night, a giant bear knocked to seek shelter. The kind girls swept the snow off his coat and played playfully, while the bear teased them not to beat their wooer dead. The bear stayed all winter but departed in spring to guard his royal treasures from greedy underground dwarfs, who could only dig through the ground when the earth thawed.

Later, while collecting firewood, the sisters found an ungrateful dwarf whose beard was stuck in a crevice of a felled tree. Snow-White used her scissors to free him, but the dwarf responded with grumbles and ingratitude. Soon after, they saved him twice more: first when an entangled fishing line nearly dragged him into the water with a large fish, and later when a giant eagle seized him on the heath. Each time, cutting his beard or tearing his odious coat only provoked his uncouth rants.

Finally, the sisters caught the dwarf admiring his stolen sparkling jewels. Suddenly, the bear emerged from the forest and struck the wicked creature down. Instantly, the bearskin fell off, revealing a handsome prince! He explained that the dwarf had bewitched him and stolen his treasure. Freed from the spell, the prince married Snow-White and his brother married Rose-Red, living happily ever after with their mother.

About the Authors

Jacob (1785–1863) and Wilhelm Grimm (1786–1859) were German scholars, philologists, and cultural researchers born in Hanau, near Frankfurt. Throughout their lives, they remained close friends and brothers, studying law together at Marburg University.

While Jacob focused on historic German linguistics, Wilhelm dedicated his work to folk poetry and storytelling. Together, they collaborated on compiling the German dictionary and preserved centuries of traditional European oral folklore. They published their legendary collection, Nursery and Household Tales, in two volumes in 1812 and 1814.

Originally compiled as an academic effort to preserve cultural heritage rather than children's books, their stories gained widespread popularity. English translations in the 1820s adapted these fairy tales for younger readers, turning works like Snow-White and Rose-Red into timeless classics.
