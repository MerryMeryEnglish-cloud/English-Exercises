Demonstratives: This, These, That & Those

Learn how to point out people, objects, and ideas clearly depending on distance and number.

How Demonstratives Work

In English, demonstratives tell us two essential pieces of information at the same time: whether an item is near or far from the speaker, and whether it is singular (one) or plural (two or more). Use the visual guide below and listen to the examples to master statements, questions, and short answers.

THIS → Singular + Near (Here)
THESE → Plural + Near (Here)
THAT → Singular + Far (There)
THOSE → Plural + Far (There)
Singular • Near

This

1 Item • Within Reach
1. This is a pencil.
2. This is my mobile phone on the desk.
Question Formula & Short Answers
Is + this + singular noun / complement?
Q: Is this your notebook?
✔ Yes, it is. ✘ No, it isn't.
Plural • Near

These

2+ Items • Within Reach
1. These are pencils.
2. These are the quarterly reports for today's meeting.
Question Formula & Short Answers
Are + these + plural noun / complement?
Q: Are these your wireless earbuds?
✔ Yes, they are. ✘ No, they aren't.
Singular • Far

That

1 Item • At a Distance
1. That is a pencil.
2. That is the new printer by the window.
Question Formula & Short Answers
Is + that + singular noun / complement?
Q: Is that the manager's laptop?
✔ Yes, it is. ✘ No, it isn't.
Plural • Far

Those

2+ Items • At a Distance
1. Those are pencils.
2. Those are our client folders on the top shelf.
Question Formula & Short Answers
Are + those + plural noun / complement?
Q: Are those your office keys over there?
✔ Yes, they are. ✘ No, they aren't.
