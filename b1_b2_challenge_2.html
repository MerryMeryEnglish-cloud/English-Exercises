Around the Fire: Why Slow Weekends Outdoors Bring Us Closer

Ready to play
✨ Interactive Vocabulary: Click or hover over any highlighted expression in the text below to see its B1–B2 definition and usage example.

For months, my extended family and our closest friends had been promising to organize a weekend getaway, yet busy work schedules kept getting in the way. Whenever we finally managed to catch up at a noisy downtown bistro, the evening felt rushed, and we spent half the night shouting over loud music. So last Friday, instead of going out to an overpriced restaurant, we packed our backpacks and headed to a lakeside cabin surrounded by pine forests and rolling hills.

The moment we arrived, the pace of life slowed down dramatically. While my cousins set up the tents and gathered firewood, my uncle—who is passionate about outdoor cooking—began preparing fresh salmon and seasonal vegetables over an open grill. Meanwhile, we mixed a refreshing pitcher of homemade elderflower lemonade alongside a few bottles of local craft cider. As far as I'm concerned, nothing compares to sharing a generous meal under the open sky after a five-hour hike.

Later that evening, while we were sitting around the campfire chatting about everything from childhood memories to travel plans, a lively debate broke out over whether living in the countryside is genuinely healthier than staying in a major metropolis. My sister argued that rural areas offer a far better quality of life, whereas my best friend pointed out that she would miss the spontaneous cultural life and diverse food scene of the city. Despite holding completely different viewpoints, nobody felt pressured to win the argument—we simply listened and enjoyed each other's company.

Early the next morning, before the mist had lifted from the water, we spotted a small herd of wild deer grazing quietly near the riverbank. Seeing wildlife up close reminded all of us how easily we take nature for granted during the week. By Sunday afternoon, everyone agreed that our outdoor retreat had been the most restorative weekend of the year, and we are already planning a spring kayaking trip together.
