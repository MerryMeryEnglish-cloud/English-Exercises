I live in a two-storey house on a quiet street in the suburbs. From the outside, it looks welcoming: it has a red-tiled roof, white walls and a wooden front door with a small porch where we keep a few potted plants. A paved driveway leads to the garage, where my father parks his car. At the back, there is a spacious garden with a lawn, a vegetable patch and a fence that keeps our dog, Max, from escaping. In summer, we often have barbecues on the patio, which is my favourite spot in the whole house.

When you walk in through the front door, you enter a hallway with a staircase leading upstairs. On the right, there is the living room, which is bright thanks to the large windows. It has a comfortable sofa, a fireplace and a bookshelf full of novels. Next to it is the kitchen, where my mother spends hours cooking. It's fully equipped, with a fridge, an oven and plenty of cupboards. The dining room is connected to the kitchen, and we gather there every evening to have dinner and chat about our day.

Upstairs, there are three bedrooms and two bathrooms. My bedroom is at the end of the corridor. It's not very big, but it's cosy: I have a single bed, a wardrobe and a desk beneath the window, where I study. From there, I can see the treetops and, on clear days, the mountains in the distance. There is also a small attic above the bedrooms, which we use for storage, and a basement downstairs where my brother keeps his drums.

Although it isn't a luxurious house, it is full of memories. For me, home is not just a building; it's the place where I feel safe, relaxed and truly myself.
