Edinburgh: A Traveller’s Guide

Edinburgh is the capital of Scotland and one of the most beautiful cities in Europe. It is famous for its long history, its old buildings and its friendly atmosphere. The city is quite small, so you can visit most of the main places on foot. Many travellers say that Edinburgh feels like a storybook. The airport is about 30 minutes from the centre by tram, and the city also has good bus services.

The best place to start is Edinburgh Castle. It stands on top of an old volcano, and you can see it from almost everywhere in the city. Inside, you can see the Scottish Crown Jewels and the Stone of Destiny, which was used in the coronations of Scottish kings. The views from the castle are also wonderful, so bring your camera.

From the castle, walk down the Royal Mile. This famous street connects the castle with the Palace of Holyroodhouse, where the King stays when he visits Scotland. On the way, you will find small shops, traditional pubs and interesting museums. It is a good place to buy souvenirs, such as tartan scarves or Scottish chocolate. Try to visit St Giles’ Cathedral too. It is beautiful inside, and you can enter for free, although they ask for a small donation.

If you like nature, climb Arthur’s Seat. It is a big hill in Holyrood Park, and the walk takes about one hour. The path is not difficult, but wear good shoes because it can be windy and wet. At the top, you will have a great view of the city and the sea. Many people go there at sunrise or sunset.

Edinburgh has two main areas. The Old Town has narrow streets and medieval houses, and some people say it is a little mysterious. You can join a ghost tour in the underground streets to learn about the dark stories of the past. The New Town is different. It was built in the eighteenth century, and it has wide streets, elegant houses and nice gardens. Princes Street Gardens is a calm place to relax between visits.

Edinburgh is also a city of culture and ideas. It was the home of writers like Walter Scott and J.K. Rowling, who wrote part of the first Harry Potter book in local cafés. Fans can walk around Victoria Street and Greyfriars Kirkyard, which inspired some parts of the stories. The Writers’ Museum and the National Museum of Scotland are also great options, and the National Museum is free.

Finally, think about the best time to travel. Summer is warm and busy, with the famous Edinburgh Festival in August. In winter, the weather is cold, but the Christmas markets and Hogmanay, the New Year celebration, are very special. Remember that it can rain at any time, so take a raincoat and an umbrella. Do not forget to try traditional food, like haggis or fish and chips.

Edinburgh is a city with something for everyone: history, nature, food and culture. Whatever the season, it will stay in your memory.
