🔑 4 Golden Rules to Remember

1. Subject Pronoun + Verb vs. Possessive Adjective + Noun

A subject pronoun replaces the person or thing doing the action and goes before a verb. A possessive adjective always accompanies a noun to show ownership.

"They are from Ohio, and that is their house."

2. Possessive Adjectives Never Take "-s"

In English, adjectives do not change for plural nouns. Use the exact same form whether the noun is singular or plural.

✓ their suitcases   ✗ theirs suitcases

3. People vs. Things (Singular & Plural)

Use he/his and she/her for people, and it/its for a single thing, place, or animal. In the plural, use they/their for both people and things.

"I like those chairs, but their legs are very tall."

4. Watch Out: "Its" vs. "It's"

Its (no apostrophe) = possessive adjective. It's (with apostrophe) = contraction of it is (also used for weather and time).

"Look at that dog. It's cute, and I love its fur."
