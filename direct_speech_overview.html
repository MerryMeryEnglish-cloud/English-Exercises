📖 Mastering Direct Speech at B2+ Level

Direct speech records the exact words spoken by a speaker inside quotation marks. In academic and professional English, flawless punctuation and sophisticated reporting verbs are required.

1. Quotation Marks & Capitalization Fundamental Syntax

Enclose the exact spoken utterance in quotation marks (either single '...' in British English or double "..." in American/international publishing). Always capitalize the first word of a complete spoken sentence:

  • Opening speech: The spoken statement begins with a capital letter even if it follows a reporting clause.
  • Sentence integration: A comma separates the introductory reporting verb from the direct quote.
Dr. Peterson noted, "The protocol requires absolute adherence."
Comma precedes the quotation; 'The' is capitalized.
2. Comma and Period Placement Punctuation Standard

When the reporting clause comes after the quote, standard publication rules dictate that the comma sits inside the quotation marks before the closing quotation symbol:

  • If the spoken clause is an affirmative statement, replace the period with a comma before closing quotes.
  • The reporting clause begins in lowercase (e.g., she admitted) unless it begins with a proper noun.
"We must review the laboratory specimens immediately," the lead researcher insisted.
Comma placed inside quotes; reporting verb remains lowercase.
3. Question Marks & Exclamation Points Punctuation Shifts

If the spoken sentence is a question or an exclamation, place the question mark (?) or exclamation mark (!) inside the quotation marks. Never add an extra comma:

  • Correct: "Have you completed the clinical trial yet?" the director inquired.
  • Incorrect: *"Have you completed the clinical trial yet?", the director inquired.
"Why were the parameters altered without consent?" inquired the auditor.
No auxiliary comma after the question mark.
4. Split / Interrupted Direct Quotes Advanced Structure

When a reporting clause is placed in the middle of a single spoken sentence:

  • Close the first segment with a comma inside quotes: "...,"
  • Follow with the reporting verb and another comma: rejoined the professor,
  • Reopen quotes with a lowercase letter for the continuation: "and ..."
"The initial prognosis is favorable," cautioned the oncologist, "yet ongoing surveillance remains vital."
'yet' continues the initial sentence and stays in lowercase.
5. Advanced B2+ Reporting Verbs (Say/Tell vs. Descriptive Verbs) Lexical Precision

At B2+ and C1 level, avoid repetitive use of said. Select precise reporting verbs that convey mood, attitude, and rhetorical force:

  • Admitting / Disclosing: admitted, acknowledged, confessed, conceded.
  • Insisting / Claiming: maintained, asserted, declared, argued, emphasized.
  • Questioning / Probing: inquired, questioned, queried, challenged.
  • Expressing hesitation or grievance: muttered, lamented, warned, muttered.
"I inadvertently miscalculated the dosage," the intern conceded with genuine remorse.
'Conceded' conveys formal admission of a fault.
